# How a pour-over coffee brew works

Pour-over brewing pushes hot water through a bed of ground coffee by gravity.
The water temperature is usually between 90 and 96 degrees Celsius.
A common starting ratio is 1 gram of coffee to 16 grams of water.
The first small pour, called the bloom, lets trapped carbon dioxide escape for about 30 seconds.
Grind size controls flow: finer grounds slow the water down and extract more.
If the brew tastes sour, the usual fix is a finer grind or hotter water.
If it tastes bitter, the usual fix is a coarser grind or a shorter brew.
A full brew of one cup normally takes three to four minutes.
